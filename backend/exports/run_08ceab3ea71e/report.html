1What we did

For each discount level we ran thousands of 'what-if' worlds. In each one the buyer's odds of saying yes wobble a little, and your delivery cost wobbles a little. We then ask the only question that matters: if you offered this discount again and again, how much profit would you bank on average? That is the expected profit - the chance of winning multiplied by the profit you keep. Because those worlds are a sample, we also work out how much the answer itself would wobble if we ran it again. Any discount whose expected profit is within twice that wobble of the best - or within 1% of the best profit, whichever is wider - is treated as giving the same answer. That is the range where you have genuine room to negotiate.

6What we recommend

Approve anything between 18% and 22% - 20% is the best single number. The 25% the rep is asking for is past it - that costs about $212.87M of expected profit.

Risk Minimal · 0/100Confidence: High
Risk of getting this wrong: 0 out of 100
Why
  • Any discount from 18% to 22% gives the same expected profit within the model's own margin of error, so there is real room to negotiate there.
  • Expected profit peaks at 20% ($5,019.01M).
  • The requested 25% delivers $4,806.14M - $212.87M less than the peak, which is more than the model's margin of error.
  • Win probability only improves +11 points for that giveaway.
What to do
  1. Approve any discount between 18% and 22% - they are the same answer within the model's own margin of error.
  2. Above 22% (the top of the safe range), require VP sign-off: the 25% being asked for costs about $85.15M per point of discount.
  3. If this quarter must not misfire, 0% is the safer call: it maximises the bad case rather than the average.
The trade-offYou are trading $212.87M of expected profit for +11 points of win probability.

7What we assumed & what to watch

Assumptions behind these numbers
  • List value $20,911,979,419, cost $10,066,512,144 (55.0% standard margin).
  • Consumption effects: 0.2% of value is consumed as unmetered burst you still pay for; 4 months of rollover defers revenue.
  • Win probability curve: from 35.0% at full price toward a ceiling of 95.0%, halfway at 16.6% discount (source: 20000 of your past deals).
  • Win probability uncertainty: plus or minus 5 points.
  • Cost volatility: plus or minus 6% of cost.
  • Margin floor: 12.0%.
  • Two discounts are treated as giving the same answer when the gap between their expected profits is smaller than twice the model's own margin of error at both discounts added together (about $117.09M at the best one), or smaller than 1% of the best profit - whichever is larger.
  • The range where the answer is the same is 18% to 22%, taken from the 17 discount levels we tested.
  • 10,000 simulations per discount level; seed 42 (re-running gives identical numbers).
