1What we did

For each discount level we ran thousands of 'what-if' worlds. In each one the buyer's odds of saying yes wobble a little, and your delivery cost wobbles a little. We then ask the only question that matters: if you offered this discount again and again, how much profit would you bank on average? That is the expected profit - the chance of winning multiplied by the profit you keep. Because those worlds are a sample, we also work out how much the answer itself would wobble if we ran it again. Any discount whose expected profit is within twice that wobble of the best - or within 1% of the best profit, whichever is wider - is treated as giving the same answer. That is the range where you have genuine room to negotiate.

Why
  • Only 0% stands out from the rest - the model cannot tell any neighbouring discount apart from it.
  • Expected profit peaks at 0% (-$97.46M).
  • The requested 25% delivers -$544.85M - $447.39M less than the peak, which is more than the model's margin of error.
  • Win probability only improves +52 points for that giveaway.
What to do
  1. Do not approve a discount on these numbers. At the best level we tested (0%) the deal still loses $97.46M.
  2. Fix the deal first: the average margin in your file is -18.6%, so the cost to deliver has to come down (or the price go up) before any discount makes sense.
  3. Above 0% (the top of the safe range), require VP sign-off: the 25% being asked for costs about $17.90M per point of discount.
  4. At 25% there is a 100% chance of breaching the 12.0% margin floor - add a volume or term commitment to earn the deeper discount.
The trade-offYou are trading $447.39M of expected profit for +52 points of win probability.

7What we assumed & what to watch

Worth knowing before you act

  • Your history runs at a negative average margin (-18.6%): on average each deal costs more to deliver than it sells for. Every discount therefore loses money, and the result shows the least-bad option rather than a good one. Treat this as a problem with the deal's cost or price, not with the discount.
  • At 25% discount there is a 100% chance the deal actually costs you money to deliver even if you win it.
