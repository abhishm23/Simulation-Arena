1What we did

We built a virtual market out of your accounts. Each buyer has a budget, a price sensitivity and a supplier they use today. Every quarter the rivals set a price, you answer with one of three strategies, and each buyer picks a supplier - the cheapest offer usually wins, but habit, the hassle of switching and a priority-support promise all count. We then played all 2 years out 100 times for each strategy, so every number below is an average across 100 different versions of the future, with the likely range shown around it. We then ranked the three strategies on the measure you chose and re-ran the market at a range of rival undercuts to see how far the answer travels before it changes.

3How we got there

1

The market we built

We put 500 buyers in a room with you and two rivals. They are not all alike: the most profitable accounts barely react to price, while the cheapest ones chase it hard. Today you serve 43.2% of them.

What 'Match every cut' does

Answer every rival cut on every account, immediately. Over 8 quarters it leaves your average price -19.9% versus today, serves 43.7% of the accounts, keeps 91.7% of the top tier and banks $104.12M of profit - ranked 3 of 3 on profit.

What 'Defensible niche' does

Hold list price, bundle priority support on the accounts worth keeping, and match only where the account commits multi-year. Over 8 quarters it leaves your average price -0.9% versus today, serves 28.2% of the accounts, keeps 90.1% of the top tier and banks $191.04M of profit - the best call on profit.

What 'Hold the line' does

Do nothing. Keep list price everywhere and let the chips fall. Over 8 quarters it leaves your average price +0.0% versus today, serves 18.7% of the accounts, keeps 71.3% of the top tier and banks $141.73M of profit - ranked 2 of 3 on profit.

The trade in plain numbers

Matching every cut wins +15.5 points of account share against the best call, and $86.92M less profit over 8 quarters. That is $5.61M of profit for every extra point of share.

The bottom line

Do not match every cut. Defensible niche banks $191.04M of profit over 8 quarters, and matching every cut gives up $86.92M of it. Defend the accounts worth keeping and let the price-chasers go.

6What we recommend

Do not authorise blanket price matching. Match only where the account commits multi-year, and bundle priority support instead.

What to do
  1. Decline blanket price matching. Tell sales the answer is no by default.
  2. Authorise a matched price only where the account signs a multi-year commitment and the deal still clears 12.0% margin.
  3. Bundle priority support on the top two tiers instead of cutting price.
  4. Let the cheapest, most price-sensitive accounts go, and redeploy the support cost that frees up.
  5. Re-check monthly: the answer held everywhere from 0% to 40% undercut.

7What we assumed & what to watch

Assumptions behind these numbers
  • 500 accounts from your file; each buyer keeps buying the same volume each quarter.
  • List price $100,000 per unit; margin 29.9% on average, so cost is 70.1% of list.
  • Rivals undercut your previous average price by 12.0% and close 50% of the gap each quarter, down to a floor of 20.0% off list.
  • Buyers weigh price about ten times more heavily than habit; a 6.0% priority-support promise counts like a discount of half that size, and costs 1.5% of margin.
  • 40% of top-tier accounts are willing to commit multi-year (60% of that rate in the mid tier).
  • Overheads of $5.00M a year are charged against profit.
  • The recommendation maximises profit; change it and the answer may change with it.
  • Robustness check: the rival's undercut alone moves, from 0% to 40% in 2-point steps, with 12 versions of the future at each point. Every other assumption is held where it is today.
  • 100 independent versions of 8 quarters per strategy; seed 42 (re-running gives identical numbers).
  • Columns read from your file: Account / customer ID, Account tier / segment, Annual spend with you, Price sensitivity (0-1), Who supplies them today, Margin on the account (%), Annual cost to support them.
