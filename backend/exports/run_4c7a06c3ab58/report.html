1What we did

For each discount level we ran thousands of 'what-if' worlds. In each one the buyer's odds of saying yes wobble a little, and your delivery cost wobbles a little. We then ask the only question that matters: if you offered this discount again and again, how much profit would you bank on average? That is the expected profit - the chance of winning multiplied by the profit you keep. Because those worlds are a sample, we also work out how much the answer itself would wobble if we ran it again. Any discount whose expected profit is within twice that wobble of the best - or within 1% of the best profit, whichever is wider - is treated as giving the same answer. That is the range where you have genuine room to negotiate.

6What we recommend

Approve 0% - it is the only discount the model can tell apart from the rest. The 25% the rep is asking for is past it - that costs about $3,835.12M of expected profit.

Risk High · 100/100Confidence: High
Risk of getting this wrong: 100 out of 100
Why
  • Only 0% stands out from the rest - the model cannot tell any neighbouring discount apart from it.
  • Expected profit peaks at 0% ($489.79M).
  • The requested 25% delivers -$3,345.34M - $3,835.12M less than the peak, which is more than the model's margin of error.
  • Win probability only improves +49 points for that giveaway.
What to do
  1. Approve 0% as the standard concession - it is the only discount the model can tell apart from the rest.
  2. Above 0% (the top of the safe range), require VP sign-off: the 25% being asked for costs about $153.40M per point of discount.
  3. At 25% there is a 100% chance of breaching the 12.0% margin floor - add a volume or term commitment to earn the deeper discount.
The trade-offYou are trading $3,835.12M of expected profit for +49 points of win probability.

7What we assumed & what to watch

Worth knowing before you act

  • At 25% discount there is a 100% chance the deal actually costs you money to deliver even if you win it.
Assumptions behind these numbers
  • List value $20,911,979,419, cost $19,593,528,342 (9.4% standard margin).
  • Consumption effects: 0.2% of value is consumed as unmetered burst you still pay for; 4 months of rollover defers revenue.
  • Win probability curve: from 35.0% at full price toward a ceiling of 95.0%, halfway at 16.6% discount (source: 20000 of your past deals).
  • Win probability uncertainty: plus or minus 5 points.
  • Cost volatility: plus or minus 6% of cost.
  • Margin floor: 12.0%.
  • Two discounts are treated as giving the same answer when the gap between their expected profits is smaller than twice the model's own margin of error at both discounts added together (about $38.00M at the best one), or smaller than 1% of the best profit - whichever is larger.
  • The range where the answer is the same is 0%, taken from the 17 discount levels we tested.
  • 10,000 simulations per discount level; seed 42 (re-running gives identical numbers).
