1What we did

We split your cost to deliver into its moving parts and gave each one - plus the currency - a range of yearly moves, from the small ones to the nasty ones. Instead of picking randomly, we walked 1,000 complete 3-year cash flows through those ranges on an even grid, so every slice of every range gets tested and nothing important is missed. Each run asks one question: at the end of each year, is the margin still above your floor? We then repeat the whole thing at every escalator from 0% upwards until the chance of breaking the floor falls below the 10% you said you could live with. Costs are modelled with a 'bad news is more likely than good news' curve, so surprise increases weigh more than surprise windfalls.

Restructure before signing: a 100.0% chance of breaking the floor cannot be fixed by an escalator alone.

Why
  • Expected margin falls from -50.0% in Year 1 to -76.0% by Year 3.
  • Chance of breaking the 12% floor by Year 3: 100.0% against a tolerance of 10.0% (100.0% if you count every year).
  • Margin at risk, in today's money: $1.13M of the -$4.65M you planned to book.
What to do
  1. Raise the starting margin or shorten the term - the gap between -50.0% and your 12% floor is too thin for this level of cost volatility.
  2. Move Delivery Labour to a pass-through or a capped index; it drives 24.1% of the uncertainty.
  3. Add a hard renegotiation trigger at the first year the margin would break the floor (Year 2).
The trade-offEvery point of escalator you win protects about $0 of what that money is worth today - and every point you ask for is a point buyer will not wear the escalator, take the renegotiation trigger instead: it costs nothing upfront and gives you an exit.
