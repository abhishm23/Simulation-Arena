1What we did

We went back through your past multi-line deals and found the ones where a line was discounted past 30%. In those deals we measured how often the other lines were cancelled inside 6 months, and compared it with how often those lines die anyway. That difference is the cherry-pick signal. We then rebuilt this quote 2,000 times, each time re-drawing whole past deals so the answer reflects how much history we actually have, and re-priced the basket with whatever survived. The risk score is three things you can check by hand: how far past your discount limit the ask sits (25%), how likely the offsetting lines are to vanish (50%), and how much of the profit sits in the line being cut (25%).

1

What is actually on the table

The basket is worth $900k and makes $489k if nothing is dropped. But 61.3% of that profit sits in Software - the exact line the buyer wants 35% off, which is 5 points past the discount line you set yourself.

What history says happens next

In 26 past deals where Support was discounted past 30%, the Software line was cancelled 11.5% of the time against a normal rate of 4.5% - 2.6 times more often. And they do not do it immediately - when a line dies it happens around month 3.

3

What the rebuild shows

Rebuilding this quote 2,000 times, the profit lands at $439k on average, and in an unlucky run of twenty it is $300k or worse. There is a 0.8% chance the bundle that survives is worth less than your 35% margin floor.

$50k average shortfall
4

What to do about it

The biggest driver of the score is how likely the offsetting lines are to vanish. Tie the discount to the behaviour: if the Support line is not live at 80% of committed hours by month 6, the 35% Software discount comes off and Software reverts to standard pricing - worth up to $210k back.

6What we recommend

Approve the 35% only as a conditional discount - expect to lose $50k if you do not tie it to the Support line.

Risk Low · 50/100Confidence: High
Risk of getting this wrong: 50 out of 100
Why
  • 61.3% of this deal's profit sits in Software, the line being cut by 35%.
  • Past deals like this one lose the offsetting lines 44.1% of the time (your own history).
  • Expected profit after cherry-picking is $439k, not $489k - a $50k gap.
What to do
  1. Make the 35% Software discount conditional: if the Support line is not live at 80% of committed hours by month 6, Software pricing reverts to the standard tier (recovering up to $210k).
  2. Do not let them drop one offsetting line and keep the other: make the discount conditional on the whole Hardware, Support basket surviving, not on any single line.
  3. Put the test at month 6, not at signature - past buyers who walk do it around month 3.
The trade-offWalking away from the 35% risks the deal; giving it unprotected costs about $50k. The clause lets you keep the deal and cap the loss. If the buyer refuses any condition, the mildest version is to cap Software at 30%, which still protects $30k.
