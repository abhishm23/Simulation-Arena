1What we did

For each discount level we ran thousands of 'what-if' worlds. In each one the buyer's odds of saying yes wobble a little, and your delivery cost wobbles a little. We then ask the only question that matters: if you offered this discount again and again, how much profit would you bank on average? That is the expected profit - the chance of winning multiplied by the profit you keep. Because those worlds are a sample, we also work out how much the answer itself would wobble if we ran it again. Any discount whose expected profit is within twice that wobble of the best - or within 1% of the best profit, whichever is wider - is treated as giving the same answer. That is the range where you have genuine room to negotiate.

6What we recommend

Approve anything between 18% and 20% - 18% is the best single number. The 25% the rep is asking for is past it - that costs about $37k of expected profit.

Risk Low · 25/100Confidence: Medium
Risk of getting this wrong: 25 out of 100
Why
  • Any discount from 18% to 20% gives the same expected profit within the model's own margin of error, so there is real room to negotiate there.
  • Expected profit peaks at 18% ($176k).
  • The requested 25% delivers $139k - $37k less than the peak, which is more than the model's margin of error.
  • Win probability only improves +15 points for that giveaway.
What to do
  1. Approve any discount between 18% and 20% - they are the same answer within the model's own margin of error.
  2. Above 20% (the top of the safe range), require VP sign-off: the 25% being asked for costs about $7k per point of discount.
  3. If this quarter must not misfire, 0% is the safer call: it maximises the bad case rather than the average.
The trade-offYou are trading $37k of expected profit for +15 points of win probability.
