1What we did

We rebuilt your approval process as a queue. Requests arrive the way yours do, each waits for a free approver, gets worked on for a realistic amount of time, then moves to the next step - and a request that waits too long sometimes gives up and walks. Running thousands of requests through that queue shows where the pile-up forms, how long the whole journey takes, and how many requests would still be sitting there when the quarter closes. We then re-ran the same queue with each fix in place to price them against each other. Every option is run on the same dice - same arrivals, same requests - so the difference between two options is the fix itself rather than luck.

What that costs

Only 49.3% of requests clear inside the 10-day promise, so 50.7% slip out of the quarter. At 15.0% of deal value lost per slip, that is $3.02M of revenue at risk. Nothing gives up outright, but the slowest tenth wait 17 days.

What the fixes are worth

Auto-approve requests under 10% is the best of them: median time falls from 10.1 to 6.6 days, requests inside the promise rise from 49.3% to 94.0%, and $2.65M of revenue comes back into the quarter.

The bottom line

The constraint is not effort, it is capacity at CF Approval: the work itself takes 2.7 days, the waiting takes 3.4. Add capacity or take work out of that queue - asking people to work faster will not move the number.

6What we recommend

Fix CF Approval first. Auto-approve requests under 10% releases $2.65M of revenue and takes 3.5 days out of the process.

Risk Moderate · 51/100Confidence: High
Risk of getting this wrong: 51 out of 100
Why
  • CF Approval runs at 87.4% busy and adds 3.4 days of waiting - more than any other step.
  • Only 49.3% of requests clear inside the 10-day promise, putting $3.02M of revenue at risk.
  • The work in that step takes 2.7 days; the waiting takes 3.4. It is a capacity problem, not an effort problem.
What to do
  1. Auto-approve requests under 10% - worth $2.65M of revenue released and 3.5 days off the median.
  2. Auto-approve anything under 10% discount without a CF Approval stop - that alone removes 40.0% of the queue.
  3. Publish a daily view of the CF Approval queue with a 10-day clock on each request, so slippage is visible before the quarter closes.
  4. Watch the tail: the slowest requests take 17 days. Nothing is walking away yet, but at that length the customer has already re-planned without you.
The trade-offAdding an approver costs salary; auto-approving below a threshold costs a little margin on small deals. Both are far cheaper than the $3.02M currently at risk.

7What we assumed & what to watch

Assumptions behind these numbers
  • Arrivals and timing measured from your own approval history: about 10.2 requests a week, 3 steps.
  • Hands-on time per step: Deal Desk Review about 2.1 days; CF Approval about 2.7 days; Legal about 2.4 days
  • Where your file records door-to-door time (queue included), we backed out the real hands-on time by finding the working time that reproduces the waiting you actually saw, given how many approvers you have.
  • Approvers per step: Deal Desk Review 6, CF Approval 4, Legal 5 (counted from the distinct names in your file)
  • A request that waits more than 25 days gives up with 25.0% probability.
  • Revenue at risk = 15.0% of the deal value of every request that misses the 10-day promise; average deal value $288,286.
  • Every request is modelled as passing through every step; steps that only some requests reach therefore look slightly busier than they are.
  • 5 simulated quarters of 200 requests each per option (1,000 requests in total); about 129 arrive and clear inside each 90-day quarter. Seed 42, so re-running gives identical numbers. Every option is tested against the same simulated requests, so the comparison is fair. Revenue at risk is the average per quarter.
